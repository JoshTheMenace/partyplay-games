# Blockwild

A cooperative voxel sandbox for 1–10 players. Each player has a first-person view on phone, tablet or computer; the host can play or watch the island. Survival starts empty-handed and has no forced ending. Creative supplies unlimited materials and flight.

New worlds follow familiar block-game progression: logs → planks and sticks → wooden, stone, iron and diamond tools. Choose a tool in the nine-slot hotbar to use it. The inventory offers a categorized recipe book, search, a Craftable filter and an ingredient-grid preview. Pocket recipes fit 2×2; larger recipes require a nearby crafting table. Furnace smelting, shared chests, beds, farming and growing saplings support a home base. Zombies, skeletons, spiders and creepers have separate behavior and original Blender models.

Desktop: click the world for mouse look, WASD move, Space jump, Ctrl sprint, Shift sneak, left mouse mine/attack, right mouse place, 1–9 hotbar, E inventory/aimed station, C crafting, M map, R food. Escape releases the mouse. Creative adds F flight, Space up and Q down. Touch works in portrait and landscape: use the left joystick, push it to the rim while moving forward to sprint, drag the world to look, and hold the labeled actions on the right. Sneak is a toggle that clears when opening a menu, rotating or leaving the page. Items opens inventory, crafting, map and help; the hotbar carousel shows about five of nine slots and scrolls sideways by swipe, wheel or trackpad. Sneak/Fly sits beside Items, leaving a two-row action cluster. Selecting a slot, reloading or rotating keeps the active slot visible. Mouse players see the hotbar without touch controls. Choose a hotbar slot and then an inventory item to assign it. The hotbar layout persists per player and round.

The host's Room menu contains Save world, Load world and Finish session. Autosaves run every 30 seconds; downloaded files provide a permanent copy. Resume with the same player names and join order to recover inventory ownership. New worlds use rules/terrain version4. Old versions1–3 preserve their terrain and legacy recipes and capacities.

Inventory holds64 of each item type, one per tool; old worlds retain999. The finite world is128×128×48 with16,384 changed cells. Capacity failures preserve crafting ingredients, mined yields and unrecovered cache contents. This is a focused sandbox rather than complete Minecraft parity: see [the researched comparison and explicit limits](BEDROCK-REFERENCE.md) and [survival details](SURVIVAL.md).

`model.ts` defines stable IDs, recipe patterns and views. `server.ts` validates inputs, runs simulation and saves; `bedrock.ts` contains new resource/tool/tree rules; `mobs.ts` handles combat. `survival.ts` owns stations and homes. `client.tsx`, `workbench.tsx`, `items.tsx` and `hotbar.ts` compose the interface. `scene.tsx` and `meshes.ts` render the world and the authored assets in `art/`.

Validation: `node --import tsx --test packages/games/blockwild/tests/*.test.ts`, `npm run typecheck`, and scoped oxlint. Use a unique `npm run build:isolated -- <name>` for browser QA; never rebuild served assets. The current task's evidence is in the platform checkout at `output/blockwild/bedrock/` and `output/playwright/blockwild-bedrock/`. No Git publication or deployment is implied.

Sound effects play on each playing device, with nearby world sounds positioned around that player. The watching display plays background music without player sound effects. See [SOUNDS.md](SOUNDS.md) for coverage, browser lifecycle behavior, asset sources and the original sound generator. The four user-supplied tracks form a repeating background playlist on the host, while sound effects stay on playing devices.
