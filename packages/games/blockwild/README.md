# Blockwild

A first-person voxel sandbox for 1–64 players in a shared PartyPlay room (it is tuned and measured at 32; 64 is the room's ceiling), in three modes: **Survival** and **Creative** in a boundless wild world, and **Caper City**, an open-world city of residents, vehicles, gigs and mischief (its own section below). The rest of this introduction is the wild. Every playing seat (phone, tablet, computer, or a host who plays) gets its own first-person world. A watching TV shows a cinematic spectator camera with the roster (cards for up to five players, a slim side column up to twelve, then a wall of name chips with a spotlight on the player on camera), the day clock and world stats. Every start grows a new random world unless the host picks a fixed seed in Settings. Survival starts empty-handed next to a tree on a 4096×4096×128 world and leads from punching trees to diamond tools, then on to villages, armor, redstone and the Nether, and finally the End: defeat the Ender Dragon to beat the game (the world carries on as a sandbox afterwards). A **Next goal** chip guides new players. Creative gives flight, instant breaking and a block palette. Sessions never end on their own (a timed Caper City round does): the host saves, loads and finishes them from the Room menu.

The design contract is [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) (protocol, block/item ids, rules, file ownership).

## Playing

The world has 12 biomes: ocean, beach, river, plains, forest, birch forest, taiga, snowy tundra, desert, mountains and snowy peaks. It also has winding caves and ravines, ores by depth (coal, iron, gold, diamond), trees that never break at chunk borders, flowers, sugar cane, pumpkins and melons. Days last 20 minutes; nights are dark, and hostile mobs spawn in darkness. Torches, lanterns and lit furnaces glow with smooth, faintly flickering light. The sun lights the faces turned towards it (long and warm at dusk), a round sun and a moon with phases cross the sky, bodies cast soft shadows, and fireflies, pollen and falling leaves fill the air.

- Survival: health, hunger, air, fall damage, cactus, drowning. Toasts warn at dusk and nightfall. Death shows a message; respawn at your bed or the world spawn (keep inventory is on by default). Spawning, respawning and reconnecting give 5 s of protection, and monsters camping the respawn point despawn. On the first day no monster spawns within 32 blocks of the world spawn.
- Crafting: MC-style shaped/shapeless recipes in the 2×2 inventory grid or a 3×3 crafting table, plus a recipe book with tabs, search, a "Can craft" filter, tap-to-craft and craft-all. Furnaces smelt while nobody watches; chests hold 27 shared slots. Beds skip the night once everyone is asleep, or half the players in a group of four or more (Leave bed, Jump or Sneak gets up).
- Building: slabs merge, stairs, logs and furnaces orient by the face you click or the way you look, doors and beds take two blocks, torches attach to walls. Farming uses a hoe, seeds, watering and bone meal; saplings grow into the same trees worldgen makes, and buckets move water.
- Mobs: zombies, skeletons, spiders and creepers at night or in caves; cows, pigs, sheep (shear them) and chickens that follow food and breed.
- Exploring: villages (oak, spruce or sandstone) with houses, a blacksmith, a library, farms and lamp posts; desert temples with a hidden TNT trap under the treasure room; mineshafts with cobwebs and spider spawners; mossy dungeons with monster spawners. Their chests roll loot once, the first time anyone opens them.
- Villagers: five professions with their own outfits and 3–5 emerald trades each (a rare diamond deal for some armorers and toolsmiths). They wander by day, go home at night, run from monsters, and restock at dawn. Zombies hunt them.
- Armor: leather, gold, iron and diamond with Minecraft's damage reduction and wear, shown on your player, in the inventory and as a HUD bar.
- Redstone: levers, buttons, pressure plates, dust, torches, repeaters, lamps, pistons and sticky pistons, iron doors and TNT, close enough to Minecraft that its tutorials work.
- The Nether: pour water on lava for obsidian, build a frame (2×3 to 21×21 inside), light it with flint and steel and stand in it for 4 seconds. The other side is a lava-sea cavern world (8 blocks of overworld per Nether block) with glowstone, quartz, zombified piglins that mob you if you hit one, and ghasts whose fireballs you can hit back. Flint comes from digging gravel (1 in 10).
- The endgame: Nether fortresses (one always near the spawn portal's Nether side) with blaze spawners → blaze rods; endermen (look at their face and they attack) drop ender pearls, which you can throw to teleport; craft eyes of ender and throw them to follow the way to one of three strongholds 640–1150 blocks out; fill the 12 portal frames and jump in. The End is a floating island over the void with ten obsidian pillars topped by healing crystals. Break the crystals, then defeat the Ender Dragon (it circles, dives, spits acid and perches on the fountain) for a victory celebration on every screen, the exit portal home and a dragon egg.
- Quartz: pillars, stairs, slabs, smooth quartz, bricks and chiseled quartz; observers (pulse when the block they watch changes) and daylight detectors for redstone; the mason villager trades for it.

### Desktop controls

Click the world to capture the mouse. **WASD** move, **Space** jump (double-tap to fly in creative), **Shift** sneak, **Ctrl** or double-tap **W** sprint. Hold **left mouse** to mine or attack; **right mouse** places or uses (hold to eat or draw a bow). **1–9** or the wheel pick a hotbar slot, **Q** drops (Ctrl+Q drops the stack), **E** opens the inventory (the creative palette in creative), **Esc** releases the mouse and opens the menu. If the browser refuses pointer lock for two world clicks in a row (some embedded frames or policies), play continues with a free cursor (a later successful lock returns to normal play): drag with any mouse button held to look, and use the middle button to look without acting.

### Touch controls (landscape)

A floating joystick appears wherever the left thumb lands; push it to the rim while moving forward to sprint. Drag anywhere else to look. Buttons: **Jump** (double-tap to fly in creative, hold to rise), **Sneak** (a toggle; in creative it is **Down**), **Mine** (hold), **Use** (tap to place or use, hold to eat or draw), **Items**, **Drop** (hold: one item after a moment, the whole stack if you keep holding; taps do nothing) and **☰** menu. Tap hotbar slots to select. In containers, tap to pick up or place, long-press to split, and switch on **Quick move** for shift-click.

## Caper City

Pick **Caper City** under Mode in the game rules (with a round of 10, 20 or 30 minutes, or free play, and whether players may bonk each other). Everyone lands on the town hall plaza of a pre-built city: a 10 × 10 grid of blocks on an island, with office towers, rows of shops, flats, houses, warehouses, a park, a market, a funfair, a beach with a pier, an airfield and a small island with a lighthouse out in the bay. The tone is cartoon mischief: bonks and stars, never anything worse.

- **Residents.** 672 named people live here. Each has a home, a job and a day: keepers open their counters at 06:30 and the late shift closes at 02:00, office workers go out for lunch, officers of the Watch walk their beats, couriers do their rounds, and by night the streets empty. Look at someone to see who they are and what they are doing; talk to them and they stop. Shops are open exactly while a keeper stands at the counter.
- **Vehicles.** City bikes (free to ride) and scooters wait on the plaza, cars and vans are parked along the streets, and traffic drives past (it brakes for you and sounds its horn). Walk up to a vehicle and press the context button (**F**) to get in; a friend can take a passenger seat. The stick or WASD steers, Jump brakes, F gets out. There are sixteen kinds, from the city bike to the Zinger coupé, with a bus, a fire engine and an ambulance at their stations, a helicopter and a small plane at Gullwing Field, and a dinghy and a speedboat moored along the pier (the only way, short of flying, to Gull Rock and its lighthouse out in the bay). A vehicle bought at Wheel Deal Motors, the airfield or the bait hut is yours: call it from the map.
- **Coins.** Job boards (the corkboards on the depot, diners, cab stands, the town hall, the clinic, the bus depot and the fire brigade) offer three gigs at a time: parcel rounds, hot food runs, cab fares, **bus routes** (four stops in a Trundler bus; friends riding along raise the pay), **ambulance runs** (fetch a patient in the Patch Wagon) and **fire calls** (park a fire engine by the blaze, or lob water balloons at it). Follow the beacon and the yellow diamond on the map. Or **fish** (buy a rod at the bait hut on the pier head, face any water, Use to cast and Use again the moment the float dips; the hut and the diners buy the catch), **busk** (hold Use with a kazoo where people are about), hit a **stunt ramp** on the quays at speed, or look for the forty **golden feathers**. Spend coins on food (it mends hearts), gadgets, hats, vehicles and boats, a tune-up or a respray, a flat, a plot of land to build on (the only place blocks can be placed or broken; the hardware shop sells them), a **business** (any shop's counter sells the shop: it pays you every minute and a cut of what other players spend there), or to pay off heat at the Watch House.
- **Mischief and Heat.** **Bonk** (the Mine button) bonks whoever is under the crosshair: residents see stars and drop a few coins, most run and the brave bonk back. Borrowing a parked car, pulling a driver out, running someone over, throwing a cream pie or emptying a till (hold Bonk on it) are all mischief too. If a resident or an officer *sees* it your Heat rises through four tiers and the Watch comes: first a ticket, then a chase on foot, then cruisers. Break their line of sight for eight seconds and it drains; a banana peel underfoot, a puff of smoke or a respray helps. Caught means the **lock-up**, a cage outside the Watch House: pay the fine, take community service (no fine, but your next gig is unpaid), sit it out for half, or have a friend work the lock and spring you. Knocked out means a clinic bill.
- **Together.** Drive up to a chequered flag post and start a **street race**: others have twenty seconds to join. The most wanted player carries a **bounty** anyone can collect with a bonk. When the **Golden Gull** lands, whoever holds it earns coins every second until somebody bonks it off them. The **bank vault** opens in twenty-two seconds for one pair of hands and half that for two, and stays quiet only while a friend holds the alarm bell outside. The yellow button on another player hands them ten coins. Every minute or two the city starts something by itself: a van sheds its parcels, a crate comes down by parachute, a shop goes half price, the lamps go out, a fire breaks out.
- **People with errands.** Seven characters (Granny Marrow in the park, Mayor Plumley on the plaza, Old Sprocket at the scrapyard, Fingers McGraw in the market, Captain Peg at the Watch House, Dockmaster Brine on the pier and Madame Fizz at the funfair) each have three or four errands, each a little more than the last. A green mark hangs over anyone who still has one for you.
- **The funfair.** Three stalls (stop the marker at the right moment) pay in tickets; the prize booth by the tent swaps tickets for candy floss, a plush gull, or a hat nobody sells.
- **Finding your way in.** The **To do** card on the HUD always lists three things worth trying next; tap it for the City Passport, which has a stamp (and a few coins) for each of 32 things and says how to get it. That is the tutorial.
- **The round.** The TV shows the city map with everyone and everything on it, what is going on right now, the most wanted, a board that turns (richest, most stamps, best angler, race wins, longest jump) and the news, with a banner for each new headline. A timed round ends by itself; either way the City Ledger ranks everyone by net worth and hands out awards to whoever earned them (Tycoon, Most Wanted, Model Citizen, Slippery, Road Hog, Bonk Champion, Jailbird, Sky High, Master Angler, Speed Demon, Daredevil, Maestro, Good Egg, Bounty Hunter, Globetrotter, Magpie, Gull Whisperer).

Extra controls in the city: **F** (or the yellow button) does what the prompt says, **G** uses the spot a vehicle is standing on (a race start, the spray pad), **H** sounds the horn, **M** (or a tap on the minimap) opens the map. On touch the stick steers a vehicle and the right-hand buttons become **Brake** (or **Up** and **Down** in the air). Stepping out of an aircraft in the air opens a parachute; so does a long fall with a parasol in your pockets.

How it is built, in short: the city and its residents are pure functions of the seed shared by the server and every screen (`src/shared/city`), so the crowd costs no traffic and is never saved; only residents knocked off their script, vehicles and each seat's own state travel. A driver's own screen simulates the vehicle and the server checks the result against that vehicle's envelope, exactly as it does for walking. [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md#caper-city-the-third-mode-mode-city) has the details.

## Architecture

Server-only code lives in `src/sim/`. The browser never imports it, and `tests/boundaries.test.ts` enforces that.

| Area | Files | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Platform entry | `src/manifest.ts`, `src/server.ts`, `src/client.tsx` | Manifest (20 Hz sim, snapshot cache of `edits`, save/finish), `rules`, and the client module (lazy `SceneView`, HUD, TV, lobby views). |
| Shared rules | `src/shared/` | Block and item tables, shapes, physics (`stepBody`), raycast, protocol (Input/View/PrivateView, `parseInput`), mining, placement, inventory clicks and recipes. The server runs these authoritatively and the client runs the same functions for prediction. |
| World generation | `src/shared/{noise,worldgen,trees,nether,loot}.ts`, `src/shared/structures/` | Deterministic `generateChunk(seed, cx, cz)` (integer hashes and `+ - * /` only, so V8 and JavaScriptCore agree) and `findSpawn(seed)`, memoized. The server and the scene use the same spawn. The Nether is a sealed corner of the same world (`x ≥ 3584, z < 512`) with its own generator, and the End is the opposite corner (`x ≥ 3584, z ≥ 3584`) with a void below y 0; structures are planned once per seed and painted chunk by chunk. |
| Simulation | `src/sim/` | `game.ts` (create/tick/presence, chunk streaming, the TV's followed seat), players and movement validation, commands, mobs and pathfinding, combat, containers, growth, views (what each screen is sent), the edit journal (`journal.ts`) and saves, plus one module per expansion system: `structures` (loot, spawners), `villagers`, `armor`, `redstone`, `portals`, `fire` and `nether-mobs`. |
| Engine | `src/client/engine/` | `VoxelEngine`: a Web Worker generates chunks, floods sky/block light and builds meshes with AO. The main thread streams columns nearest-first within a per-frame upload budget and keeps the edit layers (journal, server tail, optimistic overlay). Custom shaders give smooth light with sun-direction face shading, haze and fog, water, sway, sky dome (round sun, moon phases), clouds, the block outline and cracks. |
| Art | `src/client/art/`, `art/` | 16×16 pixel-art textures and item icons painted at runtime into a texture array. Blender scripts (`art/build_models.py`) build the GLB rigs in `public/games/blockwild/models/`, which face -Z with pivot nodes at the joints. |
| Game client | `src/client/scene.tsx`, `src/client/game/`, `src/client/audio/` | The frame loop owns the only `setInput` call and the command queue. Local physics at 1/60 s steps, targeting, mining, optimistic edits (overlay reverts on reject), predicted containers, first-person hand, remote entities through `SnapshotBuffer` with motion-driven animation and ground shadows, particles and ambient life, positional sound and host music. `feed.ts` decodes what the server sends (the scene near this seat, the bag, the roster). The TV gets a spectator camera. The world is cached by seed, so a reconnect, a loaded save or a replay of the same seed reuses its chunks. |
| UI | `src/client/ui/`, `src/client/style.css` | HUD, containers, recipe book, creative palette, menus, touch controls, TV overlay, settings, instructions and the results journal. It talks to the game only through `src/client/store.ts` and `game/predict.ts`. |

**Networking in two paragraphs.** Each screen is sent only what is near it. A playing seat's private view carries the players, mobs, items, arrows and effects around that seat as compact number tuples (every snapshot in rooms of up to 16, every second snapshot above), and its inventory only when it changed. The public view, which every socket receives, holds the world clock, the cached edit journal with a short tail of recent edits, a slim roster a few times a second and a capped feed for the watching TV. That keeps a phone's traffic from growing with the square of the roster.

Your own position is client-authoritative: the server checks each claimed move against speed and collision and teleports you back (`tp`) if it is impossible. Every action (break, place, click, craft) is a numbered command carried inside held input, because reliable actions are capped at 256 per round. The server runs each command once, in order, and reports the highest number it handled (`ack`). The client shows the result immediately and drops its guess once the ack arrives, so a rejected edit quietly snaps back.

**Saves.** Format `blockwild` version 11 for survival and creative worlds (versions 9 and 10 still load; a Caper City world is version 13, which adds each player's coins, property, career, passport, standing and businesses in 28 numbers and holds nothing of the city itself; version 12, the first city format, still loads): seed (always the real one)/worldId/mode/difficulty/keepInventory, time, the edit journal packed and deflated (up to 65,536 distinct cells as long as it packs into 100,000 bytes: tidy building reaches the ceiling, scattered single cells stop near 32,000 and never before 16,384), up to 96 players matched by exact unique name (with worn armor), chests (≤ 64), furnaces (≤ 32), animals (≤ 160), villagers (≤ 64, with trade uses), a populated-chunk bitset, the End (dragon, crystals, exit portal), the victory and stats. Redstone, portals and fire rebuild from the edits on load. The worst legal save stays under the 256 KiB limit. Older saves (versions 1–8, made before the current terrain generator) are rejected with "This world was made with the previous version of Blockwild and cannot be loaded."

## Big rooms

Measured on a heavily loaded shared development machine (load average 40–80 on 12 cores), so read the figures as relative. Players packed into one spot is the worst case for traffic, players spread out the worst for terrain.

| Room | Server process | Each phone | The TV | Before this design |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32 players in one spot, real server and sockets | about 25% of a core | 63–68 KiB/s | 23 KiB/s | about 200 KiB/s a phone (benchmark) |
| 64 players in one spot, real server and sockets | about 45% of a core | 79–86 KiB/s | 25 KiB/s | about 351 KiB/s a phone (benchmark) |
| 32 creative players flying apart at full speed | about 50% of a core, snapshots falling to 13–16 a second | 12–15 KiB/s | 11 KiB/s | ticks of 19 ms median, 96 ms at the 95th percentile |
| 64 players spread out, standing (benchmark, no server) | tick 7 ms median, 25 ms at the 95th percentile, with 3,142 chunks and 248 mobs loaded | 21 KiB/s | | 372 KiB/s a phone |

What gives out first beyond this: terrain generation when many players travel in different directions at once (the server generates a 64 KiB chunk in 1–8 ms and each player keeps 49 of them loaded: about 200 MiB for 64 players who have all walked off alone), then the traffic of one dense crowd, which still grows with its size.

Caper City is far lighter on the server than the wild (no terrain noise, no mobs; the crowd is a function of the clock). Same machine, `tools/scale-bench.ts --mode city` (no server):

|---|---|---|---|
| 32 players spread over the city | 1.0 ms median, 5.5 ms at the 95th percentile | 19 KiB/s | 1,379 chunks, 324 vehicles (104 in traffic) |
| 64 players spread over the city | 1.2 ms median, 3.5 ms at the 95th percentile | 22 KiB/s | 1,906 chunks, 310 vehicles |
| 32 players on the plaza | 0.3 ms median | 33 KiB/s | 49 chunks, 31 vehicles |
| 64 players on the plaza | 0.4 ms median | 47 KiB/s | 56 chunks, 30 vehicles |

The same rooms on a real server (an isolated build, `tools/room-host.ts` standing in for the TV, socket bots with the Blockwild hook for the seats; four minutes each, on the same shared machine while it was busy with other work). The bots walk to their own corner of the city and every other one gets into a parked vehicle on the way and drives:

| Room | Server process | Each phone | The display |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32 players spread over the city | 27 % of one core (18–36 % in five-second windows) | 21–24 KiB/s (37 KiB/s for the first half minute, everyone still on the plaza) | 13 KiB/s, largest message 4.0 KiB |
| 64 players spread over the city | 37 % of one core (29–50 %) | 25–31 KiB/s (59 KiB/s at first) | 16 KiB/s, largest message 7.5 KiB |

Twenty snapshots a second held throughout, dipping to 17 when other work took the machine. A real phone page in those rooms (headless Chromium with software WebGL, 844 × 390) received 23.8 KiB/s at 32 and 25.7 KiB/s at 64, and a real TV page 12.6 KiB/s at 32.

After the second helping (2026-11-23; the same method on a build of this tree, four minutes each, the machine at a load average of about 30 from other work; one seat in each room was a real phone page at 844 × 390, the rest socket bots that drive, race, hand each other coins and sit out the lock-up):

| Room | Server process | Each phone | The display |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32 players spread over the city | 28 % of one core (21–45 % in five-second windows) | 23–24 KiB/s to a bot, 25–27 KiB/s to the real page (33–40 KiB/s for the first half minute) | 13 KiB/s, largest message 4.5 KiB |
| 64 players spread over the city | 47 % of one core (38–74 %) | 27–29 KiB/s to a bot, 29–32 KiB/s to the real page (52 KiB/s at first) | 16 KiB/s, largest message 8.0 KiB |

So a phone is sent what it was sent before (the new things travel in the bag when they change and in the once-a-second scoreboard), and the server works harder at 64: ten points of a core more, for bounties, races, holds, events and the passport being looked at for every seat every tick. `tools/scale-bench.ts --mode city` reads as it did: 1.0 ms a tick and 19 KiB/s a phone at 32 spread out, 1.3 ms and 22 KiB/s at 64, 34 and 47 KiB/s with everyone on the plaza. The largest message a phone received was its first, 11.1 KiB (the roster, the bag and the plaza at once).

What gives out first in the city is not the server. It is the traffic of one dense crowd (59 KiB/s to each phone with all 64 on the plaza). The results screen used to be the other: the room server repeats the whole results snapshot twenty times a second for as long as it is up, and that came to 430–570 KiB/s to each screen at 64 players in the first version, about 200 once the ledger went as numbers, and 93–125 KiB/s as measured on 2026-11-23 before the last of it was dealt with. Now the ledger is a cached field (sent once), a finished city round sends nothing of the streets, and the outcome's rows of player ids are left empty because the ledger already ranks everyone: a results message is 0.8 KiB whatever the size of the room, and a phone was measured at 16 KiB/s on the results screen at both 32 and 64 players (the display at 10). On the QA machine itself the first thing to give was the software-rendered TV page: at 64 players it could not draw a frame inside the server's ten-second heartbeat and kept losing its seat.

Tools: `tools/scale-bench.ts` (tick time and bytes per screen without a server; `--mode city` for Caper City), `tools/room-host.ts` with the platform's `scripts/qa/room-bots.ts --hook packages/games/blockwild/tools/room-bot.ts` (a real server and real sockets, no browser; in the city the bots step round street furniture, walk up to parked vehicles and drive) and the render sandbox in `dev/`.

For anything visual, the sandbox runs the real scene and HUD from a simulation in the page, with no room, lobby or loading barrier:

```sh
npx vite --config packages/games/blockwild/dev/vite.config.mjs --host 127.0.0.1 --port <free-port> --strictPort
# http://127.0.0.1:<port>/games/blockwild/?scene=pasture&time=6000&freeze=1          a pasture with animals, torches and a wall at noon
# http://127.0.0.1:<port>/games/blockwild/?view=tv&players=64&scene=crowd            the TV with a full room
# http://127.0.0.1:<port>/games/blockwild/?mode=city&time=6000                        Caper City at noon (at=x,y,z stands you elsewhere; coins=, heat=)
# also: time=<day ticks>, yaw, pitch, mode=creative, bots=still; window.sandbox = { state, tick(n), setTime(t), loading() }
```

Browser QA of the room flow uses a fresh isolated build per change (never rebuild a served run), `HOST=127.0.0.1 npm run serve:isolated -- <unique-name> <free-port>` and headless Chromium with software WebGL. World loads re-upload the whole cached world into a new GL context, which takes most of a minute under software rendering; the manifest allows 90 s. Software rendering can judge visuals but not frame rate or feel. Real phones, night-time play and full rosters on real Wi-Fi still need human sessions.

Caper City under software rendering: a room page draws a frame every second or two at best and far fewer on a busy machine, and the server drops a screen that is silent for ten seconds, so keep sheets shut in room pages (their blurred backdrop over the 3D scene is what stalls longest) and take counters, job boards, the map, pockets, Heat, arrests and the clinic from the sandbox, where `window.sandbox.state` is the live simulation: move seat 0 by assigning its position and a new `tp`, set `city.players[0].heat`, and so on. Movement on a slow page is in slow motion (the physics step is capped per frame); a 640 × 360 sandbox page with `freeze=1` draws fast enough to drive and fly for real.

Playing the city's second helping in real rooms (2026-11-22/23) was done from scripts: one real phone page at 568 × 320 with touch emulation, a socket host standing in for the TV (so the phone page has the machine to itself; the real TV page has its own run), and a protocol bot for the second seat. Each scene takes the room's save, stands the players where it needs them (position, pockets, the 28 numbers of the city record), loads it back, and then uses the real controls: the stick keys to walk and steer, a drag to look, the yellow button, the sheets. What that showed that the sandbox could not: the first toast of a session was being dropped, a stamp's notice replaced the message of whatever earned it, a called vehicle found no bay, a boat's passenger stepped out onto a lamp post, a quick car stopped dead on a stunt ramp's second step, and on a phone that is not in full screen (the room's header stays, and iPhones cannot leave it) the game has about 245 px of a 320 px screen, where Sneak sat on top of Items. All fixed; the tests that would have caught the rule-level ones are in `tests/city2.test.ts`. A script that drives has to keep right (traffic comes down the middle), go round what it meets, and not stop at every corner, or a timed gig runs out in the slow motion of a software-rendered page. Even so, with the machine at a load average of 30 the page ran at about a third of real time against the server's clock (3.7 frames a second at half render resolution, 7.3 at a quarter), and the timed vehicle gigs kept running out. When the machine calmed the cab fare and the fire call were driven to their pay in a real room; the ambulance run was finished only in the sandbox, where the client and the rules share the page's clock, and the bus route was not finished in any browser (two of its four stops counted in the sandbox, one in a real room, before the script's driving wedged the bus against a kerb). The parcel round, the street race, the stunt ramp and everything untimed were played in real rooms.

Tips for driving it: headless Chromium refuses pointer lock, so click the world twice to enter the free-cursor fallback (then a middle-button drag looks without acting). Special states (villages, trading, redstone, portals, the Nether, a fight) are easiest to reach by editing a downloaded save and loading it from the Room menu, or by PUTting it to `/api/round/save` with the host's credentials. Seed 11 spawns on a beach meadow at (2056, 68, 2040) with a village 226 blocks away, a desert temple, mineshafts and dungeons nearby.
